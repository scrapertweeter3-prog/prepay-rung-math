The monthly fee hides the discount you already earned

Poket Dev's pricing page leads with $5,995 a month. The quarterly rung sits at $5,495 and the yearly one at $4,995, and the gap between them is the subscription billing itself, not a markup. Multiply it out and the quarterly plan is $65,940 a year against $71,940 on monthly, so committing four months at a time returns $6,000 a year. The yearly plan is $59,940 against the same $71,940, which returns $12,000, and $12,000 is almost exactly two months free.

Every rung keeps the same working terms. One active request at a time on a Trello board, unlimited request cards, unlimited revisions, cancel anytime, code delivered to a private GitHub repo you keep. Paying less means paying further ahead, nothing else. The $5,495 rate only pays off if you would have kept the subscription through the quarter anyway, and the $4,995 rate only pays off across a full year of steady requests, so the discount is a bet on your own roadmap.

Run it as a break-even. The quarterly rung wins once you clear four consecutive months, since four monthly cycles cost $23,980 against $10,990 for two quarterly payments. The yearly rung wins somewhere past five and a half months of use, which is where a $59,940 annual bill starts to beat the monthly cycles it replaces. Past that point the committed and the casual buyer pay different prices for the same pipeline.

The honest way to read the page: price the roadmap first, then pick the rung that matches how long the work queue will actually stay fed. The math behind the Poket Dev subscription, and the unlimited software development subscription terms behind every number here, live on the pricing page itself.
